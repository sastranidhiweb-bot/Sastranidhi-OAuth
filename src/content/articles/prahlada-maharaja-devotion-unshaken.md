Prahlāda was the son of the powerful king Hiraṇyakaśipu, who hated Lord Viṣṇu. Yet from childhood Prahlāda was devoted to Viṣṇu, having heard of Him while still in his mother’s womb from the sage Nārada.

## Tested again and again

The Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam tells how his father tried every means to turn him, and then to destroy him. Prahlāda remained calm, and kept speaking to his classmates about devotion.

## Nṛsiṁha appears

When Hiraṇyakaśipu demanded to know whether the Lord was in a pillar of his palace, Viṣṇu appeared from it as Nṛsiṁha, half man and half lion, to protect His devotee.

## What Prahlāda asked for

Offered any blessing, Prahlāda asked only for freedom from desire, and for the welfare of his father. His prayers in the seventh canto are among the most loved in the Bhāgavatam.
