Spoken on the battlefield of Kurukṣetra, the Bhagavad-gītā answers a question every person eventually faces: what should I do, and why?

## Duty without anxiety

Arjuna’s crisis is not cowardice but confusion. Kṛṣṇa’s answer begins with action itself: do what is right, and let go of the anxious grip on results. This is not indifference; it is freedom from the fear that paralyses good work.

> You have a right to perform your duty, but never to its fruits. (Bhagavad-gītā 2.47)

## Knowing the Self

The Gītā places duty within a larger vision. The Self is not born and does not die; seeing this, one acts with steadiness rather than agitation.

## Devotion as the culmination

Through its eighteen chapters, the Gītā draws the paths of action, knowledge and meditation together into devotion, offering every act to the Supreme.
