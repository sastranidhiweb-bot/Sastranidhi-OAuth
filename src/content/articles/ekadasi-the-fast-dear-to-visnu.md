Twice a month, on the eleventh day (ekādaśī) of the waxing and waning moon, many households fast and spend the day in prayer, reading and kīrtana. The Purāṇas describe Ekādaśī as especially dear to Lord Viṣṇu.

## How it is observed

Traditionally one avoids grains and beans for the day; some fast more strictly, taking only fruit, milk or water. The fast is broken the next morning, at the time given in the calendar (pāraṇa).

## More than a fast

The purpose is not hunger but remembrance: with less attention on eating, more is free for hearing, chanting and study. Each Ekādaśī has its own name and story, such as Śayanī Ekādaśī, when Viṣṇu is said to begin His rest on the serpent Ananta, and Utthāna Ekādaśī, when He awakens.
