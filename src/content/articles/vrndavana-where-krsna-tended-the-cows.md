On the banks of the Yamunā lies Vṛndāvana, the land of Kṛṣṇa’s childhood. The tenth book of the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam describes how He and Balarāma took the calves and cows to graze in its forests, played with the cowherd boys, and played His flute at dusk.

## A land remembered in every step

Pilgrims walk the parikramā, the path around the sacred places, often barefoot, because the dust of Vraja is itself held sacred. Places such as Govardhana Hill, Rādhā-kuṇḍa and the ghāṭas of the Yamunā are each linked to a pastime told in the Purāṇas.

## The mood of Vraja

The devotional traditions describe Vṛndāvana not as a place of awe and grandeur but of intimacy: the Lord known as a friend, a child and a beloved.

For devotees, it is the place most dear to Kṛṣṇa.
