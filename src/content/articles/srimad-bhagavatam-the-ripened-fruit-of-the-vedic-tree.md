The Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam opens by describing itself as the ripened fruit of the desire tree of Vedic literature, already tasted and made sweeter by the lips of Śukadeva.

## Twelve cantos

Across twelve cantos (skandhas) it tells of creation, of the avatāras, of great devotees such as Dhruva and Prahlāda, and in the tenth canto, of the life of Kṛṣṇa.

## Spoken on the bank of the Gaṅgā

The text is framed as Śukadeva’s answer to King Parīkṣit, who had seven days to live and asked what a person should do when facing death. The answer the Bhāgavatam gives is to hear about, and remember, the Lord.

> Nigama-kalpa-taror galitaṁ phalam (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 1.1.3)
