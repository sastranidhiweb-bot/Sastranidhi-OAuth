Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana Vyāsa is remembered as the great compiler of Vedic knowledge. Tradition holds that he divided the one Veda into four, so that it could be preserved and studied in an age of shorter memory.

## The Mahābhārata

Vyāsa composed the Mahābhārata, the great epic in which the Bhagavad-gītā is set. A well-known tradition tells that Lord Gaṇeśa wrote it down as Vyāsa dictated, on the condition that the dictation never pause.

## The Bhāgavatam

According to the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam itself, even after all this work Vyāsa felt dissatisfied, until his teacher Nārada advised him to describe the glories of the Lord directly. The result was the Bhāgavatam, which he taught to his son Śukadeva.

His appearance day, Guru Pūrṇimā, is still kept as a day to honour one’s teachers.
